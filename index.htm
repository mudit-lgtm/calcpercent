How to Calculate a Percentage

A percentage is a number expressed as a fraction of 100. The word comes from the Latin per centum, meaning "by the hundred." Percentages are used everywhere — from calculating a tip at a restaurant to tracking stock market returns, exam scores, and tax rates. Understanding how to calculate a percentage is one of the most practical math skills you can have.

Our free percentage calculator handles all standard percentage problems instantly, but knowing the formulas yourself helps you verify results and understand what each number means.

The Basic Percentage Formula

Formula
Percentage (%) = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100

Use this formula when you want to express one number as a percentage of another. If a student scores 72 out of 90 marks, their percentage score is (72 ÷ 90) × 100 = 80%.

✦ Worked Example

Question: What percentage is 45 out of 180?

Step 1: Divide the part by the whole → 45 ÷ 180 = 0.25

Step 2: Multiply by 100 → 0.25 × 100 = 25%

How to Find a Percentage of a Number

Formula
Result = (Percentage ÷ 100) × Number

This is the most common percentage question: "What is X% of Y?" To find 30% of 250, calculate (30 ÷ 100) × 250 = 75. Use our calculator above for instant answers — just enter the percentage and the number in Tab 1.

Step-by-Step: How to Calculate Percentage

  1. Identify the values: Determine what the "Part" is and what the "Whole" (total) is.
  2. Apply the formula: Divide the Part by the Whole: Part ÷ Whole.
  3. Convert to percent: Multiply the result by 100 to get the percentage.
  4. Interpret the result: Add the % symbol. The result tells you the proportional relationship between the two values.

Percentage Increase & Decrease

When a value grows from an old number to a new number, you're looking at a percentage increase. If a product price rises from $40 to $52, the percentage increase is ((52 − 40) ÷ 40) × 100 = 30%. Conversely, a percentage decrease applies when the new value is lower than the old value.

Percentage Increase Formula
% Increase = ((New Value − Old Value) ÷ Old Value) × 100
Percentage Decrease Formula
% Decrease = ((Old Value − New Value) ÷ Old Value) × 100

Percentage Change vs Percentage Difference

Percentage change always has a reference (the starting/old value), and can be positive (increase) or negative (decrease). Percentage difference, on the other hand, compares two values without a defined starting point — it uses the average of both values as the base. Use percentage change for before-and-after comparisons, and percentage difference when comparing two independent measurements.

How to Work Out Percentage Off (Discounts)

To calculate a discount, multiply the original price by the percentage discount divided by 100, then subtract that from the original price. For example, 25% off $120 = $120 − ($120 × 0.25) = $90. Our percent off calculator makes this instant for any price and discount percentage.

Discount / Percent Off Formula
Sale Price = Original Price × (1 − Discount% ÷ 100)

Percent Error — For Science & Lab Work

Percent error measures how close an experimental or measured value is to a known theoretical (accepted) value. It is widely used in chemistry, physics, and engineering labs. The formula uses the absolute difference between the experimental and theoretical values, divided by the theoretical value, multiplied by 100. A lower percent error indicates higher accuracy.

Percent Error Formula
% Error = (|Experimental − Theoretical| ÷ |Theoretical|) × 100

Real-World Uses of Percentages

Percentages appear in almost every area of everyday life. In finance, interest rates, investment returns, and tax rates are all expressed as percentages. In retail, discount labels ("25% off") and sales tax are calculated using percentage formulas. In education, exam scores and grade cutoffs use percentages. In health, body fat percentage, blood oxygen saturation (SpO₂), and nutritional information on food labels all rely on percentage calculations. Understanding how to calculate percentages fluently — or using a reliable percentage calculator — saves time and avoids costly mistakes.

According to Math Is Fun, percentages are one of the most universally applied mathematical concepts across both academic and professional settings. External resources like Khan Academy's percentage lessons offer further depth if you want to strengthen your understanding of the underlying concepts.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to the most common percentage questions

How do I calculate a percentage?+

Divide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100. Formula: % = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100. For example, 25 out of 200 = (25 ÷ 200) × 100 = 12.5%. You can also use the calculator on this page for instant results.

What is the percentage formula?+

There are several percentage formulas: Basic: % = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100. Find % of a number: Result = (% ÷ 100) × Number. % Increase: ((New − Old) ÷ Old) × 100. % Decrease: ((Old − New) ÷ Old) × 100. See our full percentage formula guide for all variations.

How do I calculate percentage increase?+

Subtract the old value from the new value, divide by the old value, then multiply by 100. Example: from 80 to 100 → ((100 − 80) ÷ 80) × 100 = 25% increase. Use our dedicated percentage increase calculator for instant results.

How do I work out percentage decrease?+

Formula: % Decrease = ((Old Value − New Value) ÷ Old Value) × 100. Example: a price drops from $80 to $60 → ((80 − 60) ÷ 80) × 100 = 25% decrease. Try the percentage decrease calculator for fast answers.

What is the difference between percentage change and percentage difference?+

Percentage change measures how much a value has changed relative to its original value — it has a clear starting point. Percentage difference compares two values without a defined reference, using the average of both as the base. Use change for before/after, and difference for comparing two independent values.

How do I calculate percent off a price?+

Multiply the original price by the discount percentage, then subtract: Sale Price = Original Price × (1 − Discount% ÷ 100). Example: 20% off $150 = $150 × 0.80 = $120. Use our percent off calculator for any price and discount instantly.

What is percent error?+

Percent error measures the accuracy of an experimental result compared to a known/theoretical value. Formula: % Error = (|Experimental − Theoretical| ÷ |Theoretical|) × 100. It is commonly used in chemistry and physics labs. Try our percent error calculator for quick lab calculations.

How do I calculate the percentage of a number?+

Formula: Result = (Percentage ÷ 100) × Number. To find 15% of 320: (15 ÷ 100) × 320 = 48. Use Tab 1 of the calculator on this page — enter 15 in the percentage field and 320 in the number field, then click Calculate.
