How to Calculate a Percentage — Complete Guide

A percentage is a way of expressing a number as a fraction of 100. The word comes from the Latin per centum, meaning "by the hundred." Percentages are used everywhere — from shopping discounts and exam scores to salary increases, financial growth rates, and scientific error measurements.

Basic Percentage Formula: % = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100

For example, if you scored 45 out of 60 on a test: (45 ÷ 60) × 100 = 75%. Use the free calculators above to solve any percentage problem instantly, or read on to understand how each formula works.

Three Types of Percentage Problems

Almost every percentage question falls into one of three categories. Understanding which type you're dealing with makes calculation straightforward:

  1. Finding the percentage — "What % is X of Y?"
    Example: What percentage is 30 of 200? → (30 ÷ 200) × 100 = 15%
  2. Finding the part — "What is X% of Y?"
    Example: What is 25% of 80? → (25 ÷ 100) × 80 = 20
  3. Finding the whole — "X is Y% of what?"
    Example: 15 is 30% of what number? → 15 ÷ (30 ÷ 100) = 50

Percentage Increase and Decrease

When a value changes over time, you'll often need to calculate the percentage change. The formula works for both increases and decreases:

% Change = ((New Value − Old Value) ÷ Old Value) × 100

A positive result means an increase; a negative result means a decrease. For example, if a price goes from $80 to $96: ((96 − 80) ÷ 80) × 100 = 20% increase. Use our dedicated percentage increase calculator or percentage decrease calculator for these specific calculations.

Percentage Discount and Sale Price

One of the most practical uses of percentages is calculating discounts. Our free percentage off calculator and discount calculator handle this instantly, but the formula is:

Sale Price = Original Price × (1 − Discount% ÷ 100)

For example, 25% off $80: $80 × (1 − 0.25) = $80 × 0.75 = $60. The saving is $80 − $60 = $20.

Percent Error in Science

In chemistry and physics, percent error measures how accurate an experimental result is compared to the theoretical or accepted value:

% Error = |Experimental − Theoretical| ÷ |Theoretical| × 100
