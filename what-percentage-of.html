What is a Percentage?

A percentage is a way of expressing a number as a part of a whole, where the whole is always taken to be 100. It is one of the most universally used ways to compare quantities — because fixing the denominator at 100 makes all proportions directly comparable, regardless of the original size of the groups being compared.

Think of a percentage as answering the question: "If there were exactly 100 of these, how many would qualify?" If 7 out of 10 students passed a test, that is 70 out of 100 — or 70%. If 3 out of 4 people prefer tea, that is 75 out of 100 — or 75%.

Core Idea

Percent = Per + Cent. "Per" means "for every" and "cent" means 100 (think century, centimetre, centime). So "percent" literally means "for every hundred." Writing 60% is the same as writing 60/100, or the fraction 3/5, or the decimal 0.60.

Percentages are not a different type of number — they are simply a standardised way of presenting a ratio. The power of the percentage lies in that standardisation: comparing "72 out of 200" with "36 out of 100" is awkward, but comparing 36% with 36% is instant. Converting both to a denominator of 100 puts everything on the same footing.

The % Symbol — History & Origin

%

How the % Symbol Evolved

The percent symbol % evolved gradually from the Italian commercial notation of the 15th century. Merchants would write "per cento" (meaning "per hundred") in financial records to express rates and proportions. Over time, this was abbreviated to "p cento", then "p 100", then the fraction-like "°/°", and eventually simplified to the familiar % symbol we use today.

The symbol consists of two zeros (representing the two zeros in 100) separated by a diagonal slash — a visual reminder that a percentage is always a fraction out of a hundred. The full evolution was complete by the early 20th century.

Related symbols: ‰ (per mille = per thousand) and ‱ (per ten-thousand / basis point), used in finance and statistics.

The Percentage Formula

Every percentage problem reduces to a single fundamental relationship between three quantities: the Part, the Whole, and the Percentage. Know any two and you can always find the third.

Core Percentage Formula
Percentage (%) = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100

This answers: "What percent of the Whole is the Part?"

Find the Part
Part = (Percentage ÷ 100) × Whole

This answers: "What is X% of a number?"

Find the Whole
Whole = Part ÷ (Percentage ÷ 100)

This answers: "X is Y% of what number?"

✦ All Three in One Example

Scenario: 24 students out of a class passed an exam.

Find %: Class has 30 students → (24 ÷ 30) × 100 = 80% passed

Find Part: 80% of 30 = (80 ÷ 100) × 30 = 24 students

Find Whole: 24 is 80% of what? → 24 ÷ 0.80 = 30 students total

Percentages, Fractions & Decimals

Percentages, fractions, and decimals are three different ways of expressing the same underlying value — a proportion. Being fluent in converting between all three is a core maths skill.

Percentage ↔ Decimal

Converting between percentages and decimals is the most common conversion and the fastest to do mentally:

  • % → Decimal: Divide by 100 (move decimal 2 places left) — 75% → 0.75
  • Decimal → %: Multiply by 100 (move decimal 2 places right) — 0.375 → 37.5%

Percentage ↔ Fraction

  • % → Fraction: Write as /100 and simplify — 40% → 40/100 → 2/5
  • Fraction → %: Divide numerator by denominator × 100 — 3/8 → 0.375 × 100 → 37.5%

Three Types of Percentage Problems

Almost every percentage calculation in real life falls into one of three types. Recognising which type you're dealing with tells you which version of the formula to apply.

TypeQuestionFormulaExample
Type 1 — Find the % What % of 80 is 20? (Part ÷ Whole) × 100 (20 ÷ 80) × 100 = 25%
Type 2 — Find the Part What is 30% of 150? (% ÷ 100) × Whole (30 ÷ 100) × 150 = 45
Type 3 — Find the Whole 18 is 45% of what? Part ÷ (% ÷ 100) 18 ÷ 0.45 = 40

Can a Percentage Be More Than 100%?

✅ Yes — Percentages Can Exceed 100%

A percentage greater than 100% means you have more than the original whole amount. This happens frequently in growth metrics, comparisons, and finance. It does not indicate an error.

Example 1 — Sales growth: Revenue grew from $200,000 to $650,000. That is 325% of the original — or a 225% increase.

Example 2 — Population: A city's population is 180% of what it was 20 years ago — meaning it grew to nearly double.

Rule of thumb: Use "% of" for total values (can exceed 100%) and "% increase/decrease" for changes (increase of 225% means it tripled — the change itself can also exceed 100%).

Conversely, a percentage can also be a very small decimal — like 0.003% (3 parts per 100,000). Percentages have no theoretical upper or lower limit; they are simply a ratio scaled to 100.

Real-World Uses of Percentages

Percentages appear in virtually every area of daily life. Their universal adoption comes from one key advantage: they put every proportion on a common scale of 100, making comparisons instant and intuitive.

Shopping & Discounts

Worked Examples

✦ Example 1 — What % of 200 is 50?

Formula: (Part ÷ Whole) × 100 = (50 ÷ 200) × 100

Step 1: 50 ÷ 200 = 0.25

Step 2: 0.25 × 100 = 25%

50 is 25% of 200.

✦ Example 2 — What is 15% of 340?

Formula: (15 ÷ 100) × 340

Step 1: 15 ÷ 100 = 0.15

Step 2: 0.15 × 340 = 51

15% of 340 is 51.

✦ Example 3 — 72 is 60% of what number?

Formula: Whole = Part ÷ (% ÷ 100) = 72 ÷ 0.60

Step 1: 60% as decimal = 0.60

Step 2: 72 ÷ 0.60 = 120

72 is 60% of 120.

✦ Example 4 — A shirt costs ₹800. It's discounted by 35%. What is the sale price?

Amount saved: ₹800 × 0.35 = ₹280

Sale price: ₹800 − ₹280 = ₹520

Or directly: ₹800 × (1 − 0.35) = ₹800 × 0.65 = ₹520

✦ Example 5 — A company's revenue grew from ₹12 lakh to ₹15 lakh. What % increase is that?

Formula: % Change = ((New − Old) ÷ Old) × 100

= ((15 − 12) ÷ 12) × 100 = (3 ÷ 12) × 100 = 25% increase

What is a Percentage — FAQs

Common questions about the definition, meaning, and use of percentages

What is a percentage in simple terms?+

A percentage is a number out of 100. It tells you what portion of a whole something represents, if that whole were divided into exactly 100 equal parts. For example, 40% means 40 out of every 100 — or 40/100 — or 0.40 as a decimal. The symbol % means "per hundred."

What does the % symbol mean?+

The % symbol means "per hundred" or "out of 100." It is shorthand for dividing by 100. Writing 55% is the same as writing 55/100, or 0.55 as a decimal. The symbol evolved from the Italian commercial notation per cento used by merchants in the 15th century and was standardised into its current form by the 20th century.

What is the difference between percent and percentage?+

In everyday use they are interchangeable, but technically: percent is used with a specific number (e.g., "35 percent" or "35%"), while percentage is used without a number to refer to the concept in general (e.g., "a large percentage of voters"). Think: "The interest rate is 5 percent" but "The percentage of students who passed increased."

How do you convert a percentage to a decimal?+

Divide by 100, or simply move the decimal point two places to the left. Examples: 75% → 0.75 | 3.5% → 0.035 | 120% → 1.20 | 0.5% → 0.005. To reverse (decimal to percent), multiply by 100 or move the decimal two places to the right: 0.68 → 68%.

Can a percentage be more than 100%?+

Yes — absolutely. A percentage over 100% simply means the value is greater than the original whole or reference. Example: if sales doubled from $500 to $1,000, the new value is 200% of the original. The increase itself is 100%. Percentages over 100% are very common in growth, finance, and comparisons.

Why do we use percentages instead of fractions?+

Percentages fix the denominator at 100, making comparisons instant and universally intuitive. Comparing "17 out of 43" with "31 out of 79" is awkward — but comparing 39.5% with 39.2% is immediate. Fractions are more precise but harder to compare quickly. Percentages also naturally express proportional change, which fractions cannot do as cleanly.

What is 100% of something?+

100% of something means the complete, entire whole — nothing added, nothing removed. 100% = 100/100 = 1. Saying "100% of $80" means $80 itself. Saying "you gave 100%" means you gave everything. It is the baseline reference point: below 100% means a portion, above 100% means more than the whole.

What is 0% and what does it mean?+

0% means none, zero, nothing of the whole. 0% of any number is always 0. In context: a 0% interest rate means no interest charged; 0% battery means completely empty; 0% probability means an event is impossible. It is the lower baseline — the empty side of the 0%–100% spectrum (or 0%–and-beyond for cases exceeding 100%).
