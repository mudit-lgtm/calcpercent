Percentage Formulas

Every percentage calculation uses one of these core formulas. Bookmark this page for quick reference.

Basic Percentage

% = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100

Example: What percentage is 45 out of 60?
→ (45 ÷ 60) × 100 = 75%

Use our percentage guide for step-by-step walkthroughs.

Percentage Increase

% Increase = ((New − Old) ÷ Old) × 100

Example: Price goes from $80 to $96:
→ ((96 − 80) ÷ 80) × 100 = 20% increase

Use our percentage increase calculator.

Percentage Decrease

% Decrease = ((Old − New) ÷ Old) × 100

Example: Price drops from $200 to $160:
→ ((200 − 160) ÷ 200) × 100 = 20% decrease

Use our percentage decrease calculator.

Percentage Difference

% Diff = |V1 − V2| ÷ ((V1 + V2) ÷ 2) × 100

Example: Comparing 40 and 50:
→ |40 − 50| ÷ 45 × 100 = 22.22%

Use our percentage difference calculator.

Percentage Off (Discount)

Sale Price = Price × (1 − Discount% ÷ 100)

Example: 25% off $80:
→ $80 × 0.75 = $60 (saving $20)

Use our percentage off calculator.

Percent Error

% Error = |Experimental − Theoretical| ÷ |Theoretical| × 100

Example: Experimental: 9.8, Theoretical: 10.0:
→ |9.8 − 10| ÷ 10 × 100 = 2% error

Use our percent error calculator.

What Is a Percentage?

A percentage is a number expressed as a fraction of 100. The word comes from the Latin per centum, meaning "by the hundred." When you say something is 75%, you mean 75 out of every 100. Percentages are a universal language for comparing proportions — whether you're reading a pay slip, checking exam results, or analysing data.

The basic percentage formula is: % = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100. For example, if you scored 45 out of 60 on a test, your percentage is (45 ÷ 60) × 100 = 75%.

Three Types of Percentage Problems

Almost every percentage question falls into one of three categories. Our percentage calculator solves all three instantly, but understanding the types helps you choose the right tool.

  1. Finding the percentage — "What % is X of Y?"
    Use this when you know the part and the whole, and need the percentage. Example: What percentage is 30 of 200? → (30 ÷ 200) × 100 = 15%
  2. Finding the part — "What is X% of Y?"
    Use this when you know the percentage and the whole, and need the actual value. Example: What is 25% of 80? → (25 ÷ 100) × 80 = 20
  3. Finding the whole — "X is Y% of what?"
    Use this when you know the percentage and the part, and need the original total. Example: 15 is 30% of what? → 15 ÷ (30 ÷ 100) = 50

How to Calculate Percentage Increase and Decrease

When a value changes over time, you calculate the percentage change using: % Change = ((New − Old) ÷ Old) × 100. A positive result is an increase; a negative result is a decrease.

For example, if a price rises from $80 to $96: ((96 − 80) ÷ 80) × 100 = 20% increase. If a price drops from $200 to $160: ((200 − 160) ÷ 200) × 100 = 20% decrease.

Use our dedicated percentage increase calculator or percentage decrease calculator for these calculations. For a general positive or negative change, use the percentage change calculator.

Percentage Difference vs. Percentage Change

These two calculations are often confused. Percentage change has a defined direction — it goes from an "old" value to a "new" value. Percentage difference is symmetric — it compares two values without a defined direction, using the average as the base.

Use percentage change for before/after comparisons (prices, growth rates, scores). Use percentage difference when you're comparing two parallel values with no inherent order (e.g., comparing two competitors' prices). Our percentage difference calculator makes this instant.

How to Calculate Percentage Discount

To find the sale price after a percentage discount: Sale Price = Original Price × (1 − Discount% ÷ 100). For 25% off $80: $80 × (1 − 0.25) = $80 × 0.75 = $60. The saving is $20.

Our percentage off calculator shows both the saving amount and the final price instantly. Useful for shopping, GST calculations, and business discounting.

Percent Error in Science

In chemistry and physics, percent error measures how accurate an experimental result is compared to a known theoretical or accepted value. The formula is: % Error = |Experimental − Theoretical| ÷ |Theoretical| × 100.

The absolute value bars ensure the result is always positive — it's a measure of accuracy, not direction. A smaller percent error means a more accurate experiment. Use our percent error calculator for chemistry and physics labs.

Working Out Percentages on a Calculator

On most standard calculators, you can find a percentage of a number by typing: [number] × [percent] ÷ 100 =. For example, for 15% of 200: press 200 × 15 ÷ 100 = 30.

Some calculators have a dedicated % key that converts the percentage for you automatically. On a smartphone or computer, our online percentage calculator is faster and shows the full formula, so you can verify your working.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to the most common percentage questions.

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

Multiply the number by the percentage, then divide by 100. For example, 20% of 150 = (20 × 150) ÷ 100 = 30. Or use our percentage calculator above — just type in the values and click Calculate.

What is the formula for percentage increase?

% Increase = ((New Value − Old Value) ÷ Old Value) × 100. If a price goes from $50 to $65, the increase is ((65 − 50) ÷ 50) × 100 = 30%. Try the percentage increase calculator.

How do I work out what percentage one number is of another?

Divide the first number by the second number, then multiply by 100. Example: What percentage is 12 of 80? → (12 ÷ 80) × 100 = 15%. Select "X is what % of Y?" in our calculator above.

How is percentage difference different from percentage change?

Percentage change requires a "before" and "after" value. Percentage difference compares two values without a defined direction, using their average as the reference. Use our percentage difference calculator when neither value is the "original."

How do I calculate percent error?

% Error = |Experimental Value − Theoretical Value| ÷ |Theoretical Value| × 100. This is used in science labs to measure accuracy. Try our dedicated percent error calculator which shows the full working.

How do I calculate percentage on a calculator?

Type: number × percentage ÷ 100. Example: For 15% of 200, press 200 × 15 ÷ 100 = 30. Our online percentage calculator is faster and shows the formula, so you can check your method too.

How do I find the percentage of marks?

Divide your marks by the total marks, then multiply by 100. Example: 72 out of 90 = (72 ÷ 90) × 100 = 80%. Our main calculator handles this with the "X is what % of Y?" option.

What does percentage off mean?

Percentage off means a discount applied to the original price. 20% off $100 means you pay $80 and save $20. Use our percentage off calculator to find the final price and savings for any discount amount.
