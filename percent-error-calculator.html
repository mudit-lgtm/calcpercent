What is Percent Error?

Percent error (also written as percentage error or % error) is a measure of how far an experimental or measured value deviates from the accepted theoretical value. It is widely used in chemistry, physics, biology, and engineering to evaluate the accuracy of experimental results.

The Percent Error Formula

% Error = |Experimental Value − Theoretical Value| ÷ |Theoretical Value| × 100%

The vertical bars | | denote absolute value — this ensures the result is always positive regardless of whether the experimental value was higher or lower than the theoretical value.

Worked Example: Density of Water

  • Theoretical (accepted) density of water at 20°C: 0.998 g/mL
  • Your experimental measurement: 0.975 g/mL
  • Difference: |0.975 − 0.998| = 0.023
  • Divide by theoretical: 0.023 ÷ 0.998 = 0.02304
  • Multiply by 100: 2.31% error

Percent Error vs Percent Difference

Percent error uses a known theoretical value as the reference. Percent difference compares two experimental values without a known "correct" answer — see our percentage difference calculator.

Significant Figures and Percent Error

In scientific reporting, percent error is typically expressed to the same number of significant figures as your measurements. If your data has 3 sig figs, report your percent error to 3 sig figs as well. Use the "Round to significant figures" checkbox in our calculator above.

Sources of Percent Error

  • Systematic errors: Consistently off in one direction (e.g., uncalibrated scale)
  • Random errors: Unpredictable variations in measurement
  • Human error: Misreading instruments or recording mistakes
  • Environmental factors: Temperature, pressure, humidity changes

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the percent error formula?
% Error = |Experimental − Theoretical| ÷ |Theoretical| × 100%
Can percent error be negative?
The standard percent error formula uses absolute values, making the result always positive. Some formulas omit the absolute value to show whether the experimental value was above or below theoretical — check with your teacher or textbook.
How do I calculate average percent error?
Calculate the percent error for each individual measurement, sum them all up, then divide by the total number of measurements. Use the "Average Percent Error" tab above.
