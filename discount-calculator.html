How to Calculate a Discount

Calculating discounts is one of the most practical uses of percentage maths. Whether you're shopping for a sale, comparing trade quotes, pricing your own products, or calculating Australian retail markdowns, knowing the real sale price and exact savings amount helps you make smarter decisions instantly.

The Discount Formulas

Two core formulas cover all discount calculations. Use whichever suits your situation:

Discount Amount = Original Price × (Discount% ÷ 100) e.g. 25% off $80 → $80 × 0.25 = $20 saved Sale Price = Original Price − Discount Amount e.g. $80 − $20 = $60 final price

Alternatively, calculate the sale price in one step by multiplying by the remaining fraction:

Sale Price = Original Price × (1 − Discount% ÷ 100) e.g. 25% off $80 → $80 × 0.75 = $60

Worked Example — Retail Discount

🛍 Example: 30% Off a $180 Jacket

  1. Original price: $180
  2. Discount: 30%
  3. Discount amount: $180 × 0.30 = $54
  4. Sale price: $180 − $54 = $126
✅ Sale Price: $126   |   You Save: $54

Worked Example — Finding the Discount Needed

🔍 Example: What Discount Gets $150 Down to $99?

  1. Original price: $150
  2. Target price: $99
  3. Difference: $150 − $99 = $51
  4. Discount %: ($51 ÷ $150) × 100 = 34%
✅ You need a 34% discount to bring $150 down to $99.

How to Calculate GST-Inclusive Discounts in Australia

In Australia, most advertised prices include 10% GST. When calculating discounts on GST-inclusive prices, you apply the discount to the full GST-inclusive amount. To find the GST component in the original price, divide by 11. For example, a $220 item includes $20 GST ($220 ÷ 11 = $20). If you apply a 20% discount: $220 × 0.80 = $176 sale price (includes $16 GST). Use our money percentage calculator for more Australian dollar calculations.

Percentage Discount vs Percentage Off — Is There a Difference?

No — "percentage discount" and "percentage off" mean the same thing. Both refer to reducing a price by a given percentage. Our percentage off calculator covers the same core calculation. The main difference on this page is the reverse calculator — the ability to find what discount % is needed to go from one price to another, which is useful for retailers, marketers, and negotiators setting prices.
