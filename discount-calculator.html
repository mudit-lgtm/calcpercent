How to Calculate a Discount

Calculating discounts is one of the most practical applications of percentage maths. Whether you're shopping at Myer, catching an Amazon Prime Day deal, or comparing trade quotes, knowing the real sale price and savings amount helps you make better decisions.

The Discount Formulas

Discount Amount = Original Price × (Discount% ÷ 100)
Sale Price = Original Price × (1 − Discount% ÷ 100)

Worked Example

  • A jacket is originally priced at $180 with a 25% discount.
  • Discount amount: $180 × 0.25 = $45
  • Sale price: $180 × 0.75 = $135
  • Or: $180 − $45 = $135

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate 20% off?
Multiply the price by 0.80. Example: 20% off $90 = $90 × 0.80 = $72. You save $18.
What discount do I need to bring a $150 item to $99?
((150 − 99) ÷ 150) × 100 = (51 ÷ 150) × 100 = 34% discount. Use the "Find % Discount Needed" tab above.
What's the difference between this and the Percentage Off Calculator?
They calculate the same thing. This page also includes the reverse calculator — "What discount % is needed to go from $X to $Y?" — which is useful for pricing decisions. See also: Percentage Off Calculator.
