How to Calculate a Discount (Percentage Off)

To calculate the sale price after a percentage discount, use the formula:

Sale Price = Original Price × (1 − Discount% ÷ 100)

For example, to find 25% off $80: $80 × (1 − 0.25) = $80 × 0.75 = $60. The saving is $80 − $60 = $20. This is the most common discount calculation and is used for everything from online shopping to trade pricing.

How to Calculate the Discount Amount

If you just need the dollar amount saved (not the final price), use:

Savings = Original Price × (Discount% ÷ 100)

Example: 30% off $150 → Savings = $150 × 0.30 = $45. The sale price is then $150 − $45 = $105. Both approaches give the same result — our discount calculator shows both automatically.

How to Find What Percentage Off Was Applied

If you know the original price and the sale price, you can work backwards to find the discount percentage:

Discount % = ((Original − Sale) ÷ Original) × 100

Example: Original $80, Sale $60 → ((80 − 60) ÷ 80) × 100 = (20 ÷ 80) × 100 = 25% off. This is the same as a percentage decrease calculation — price went down by 25%.

How to Find the Original Price from a Sale Price

This is the reverse discount calculation. If you know the sale price and the discount percentage, the original price is:

Original Price = Sale Price ÷ (1 − Discount% ÷ 100)

Example: Sale price $75, 25% off was applied → $75 ÷ (1 − 0.25) = $75 ÷ 0.75 = $100 original price. A common mistake is to add the percentage to the sale price — this gives the wrong answer. The formula above is the correct approach.

Stacking Multiple Discounts

When two discounts are applied one after another, they are not simply added together. A 10% discount followed by a 5% discount does not equal 15% off — it equals 14.5% off. The formula for stacked discounts is:

Final Price = Original × (1 − D1/100) × (1 − D2/100)

Example: $200 with 10% off then 5% off → $200 × 0.90 × 0.95 = $171 (saving $29, or 14.5%). If the discounts were simply added (15% off), the result would be $170 — a $1 difference. This matters more at larger amounts or with larger stacked discounts.

GST and Discount: Which Comes First?

In Australia, prices are typically displayed inclusive of 10% GST. When a discount is applied to the displayed (GST-inclusive) price, the discount comes off first. Example: A $110 item (including $10 GST) with 20% off:

  • Discounted price: $110 × 0.80 = $88
  • GST component in $88: $88 ÷ 11 = $8.00

The percentage calculator can help you work out the pre-GST amount — take 10% of a price using the "What is X% of Y?" mode to find the GST component.

Percentage Off vs. Percentage Decrease

A discount and a percentage decrease use identical formulas. When a price is "25% off," that's mathematically the same as saying the price has "decreased by 25%." The only practical difference is context — "percentage off" is used in retail settings, while "percentage decrease" is used more broadly for any falling value (population, temperature, revenue, etc.).

Common Discount Percentages: What They Mean

  • 5% off — Small loyalty discount or early bird offer
  • 10% off — Standard promotional or member discount
  • 15% off — Moderate sale or trade discount
  • 20% off — End-of-season sale or newsletter discount
  • 25% off — Significant sale — one quarter of the price removed
  • 30% off — Major sale, commonly seen in fashion retail
  • 50% off — Half price — clearance or closing-down sale
  • 75% off — Deep clearance — three-quarters removed

Mental Maths for Discounts

  • For 10% off, drop the last digit: $79.99 → ~$72
  • For 20% off, find 10% then double it and subtract
  • For 25% off, divide the price by 4, then subtract that
  • For 15% off, find 10% + 5% (half of 10%) and subtract both
  • For 50% off, simply halve the price
  • Stacked discounts multiply — never add percentages together

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about calculating discounts and sale prices.

How do I calculate 20% off a price?

Multiply the price by 0.80. For example, 20% off $150 = $150 × 0.80 = $120. The saving is $30. You can also find 20% of the price first ($30), then subtract it from $150. Use the calculator above for any discount percentage.

How do I work out a percentage off on a calculator?

Type: original price × (1 − discount ÷ 100). For 25% off $80: press 80 × 0.75 = 60. Or: 80 − (80 × 0.25) = 60. Our online discount calculator does this automatically with the formula shown.

What is the formula for percentage off?

Sale Price = Original Price × (1 − Discount% ÷ 100). The saving amount = Original Price × (Discount% ÷ 100). For example, 30% off $200: Sale = $200 × 0.70 = $140. Saving = $200 × 0.30 = $60.

Is 25% off the same as paying 75% of the price?

Yes, exactly. If something is 25% off, you pay 100% − 25% = 75% of the original price. So $100 with 25% off = $100 × 0.75 = $75. This is the quickest mental shortcut — subtract the discount from 100%, then multiply by that decimal.

Do stacked discounts add up?

No — stacked discounts multiply, not add. A 10% discount followed by a 5% discount equals 14.5% off (not 15%). Formula: Final = Original × (1 − 0.10) × (1 − 0.05) = Original × 0.855. The combined effect is always less than the sum of individual discounts.

How do I find the original price if I only know the sale price?

Divide the sale price by (1 − discount%/100). Example: Sale price $60, 25% off was applied: $60 ÷ 0.75 = $80 original. Use the "Find Original" tab in the calculator above for this calculation.

What is a percentage off calculator used for?

Mainly for shopping — to find the sale price during a sale, compare deals, verify a discount was applied correctly, or work backwards from a sale price to find the original RRP. Also used in retail pricing, trade discounts, and margin calculations.

How is a discount different from a percentage decrease?

They use the same formula. A "30% discount" and a "30% decrease in price" mean exactly the same thing mathematically. "Discount" is the retail term; "percentage decrease" is the broader mathematical term used for any kind of reduction. See our percentage decrease calculator.
